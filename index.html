How it works

From touchdown to shared ride in five steps

No app-store treasure hunt at the curb, no shouting your suburb at a taxi line. The whole group forms before you reach baggage claim.

STEP 1

Verify with your PNR

Scan your boarding pass or forward the booking email. The PNR proves you're a real passenger on a real flight — and it's deleted the moment it's checked.

STEP 2

We open your window

Every flight landing within ±45 minutes of yours joins one matching pool. Three flights dumping 400 people at once is a feature, not chaos.

STEP 3

Accept your group

Travelers headed your direction get an invite. You see first names and verified badges only — nobody joins your group uninvited.

STEP 4

Chat & call, privately

Message the group or one-on-one, call like WhatsApp — but numbers stay hidden and everything vanishes when the trip ends.

STEP 5

Meet, ride, split

One pinned meeting point per terminal — "Zone C, Pillar 4." The group books one Uber or Lyft together and the fare splits in the app. Done.

Privacy is the product

Sharing a car with strangers only works if people feel safe. So Landpool is built privacy-first — the chat and calls are a feature, but what they hide is the point.

  • No phone numbers, everChat and VoIP calls run inside the app, WhatsApp-style. Your real number is never shown to anyone.
  • Masked until mutual opt-inFirst name + verified-traveler badge is all anyone sees. Full profiles unlock only when both sides agree.
  • Trip-scoped, then goneGroup chat, 1-on-1 threads and call history auto-expire when the ride ends. No lingering contact with strangers.
  • Women-only pools, block & reportOpt into women-only matching at verify time. One tap blocks a rider and flags the pool to our safety team.

Why it works

Built for the three problems that kill ride-sharing

Trust: everyone in the car was on a plane

The PNR gate is the moat. BlaBlaCar can't verify a flight; Uber doesn't try. On Landpool, every rider proved minutes ago that they hold a real booking on a real flight landing at this airport.

  • Boarding-pass scan or booking-email forward — no airline API needed, works at any airport on day one.
  • PNR is checked, then deleted. It's proof, not data we keep.
  • Live trip sharing with your own contacts from meet point to drop-off.

Logistics: one pillar, one car, zero curb chaos

Airport pickups fail on the last 100 meters. Landpool gives every pool a single pinned meeting point per terminal — and the driver stays out of your chat entirely. The group coordinates here, then books one Uber or Lyft together.

  • Designated meet points — "Terminal 4, Zone C, Pillar 4" — with a photo of the exact spot.
  • Delay-proof windows: live flight tracking slides late flights into the next pool automatically.
  • One Uber or Lyft for the group — fare splits in the app, no curb-side IOUs.

Development plan

How we build and launch it

The tech is the easy part — matching, chat and payments are solved problems. The plan spends its effort where this idea actually lives or dies: density at one airport, trust, and pickup logistics.

PHASE 1 · MVP

One airport, proven flow

  • Mobile app (iOS + Android) with boarding-pass scan verification
  • Matching engine: airport + ±45-min window + destination zone
  • Group chat, masked 1-on-1 chat and in-app voice calls
  • Meet-point pins; the group books one Uber/Lyft together, fare split in-app — no drivers, no fleet
  • Launch at one high-volume airport; seed every arriving flight bank
PHASE 2 · DENSITY

Make pools form every time

  • Live flight-tracking integration — delays re-pool automatically
  • Women-only pools and verified co-traveler preferences
  • Concert mode: ticket-verified pools for concerts, games and festivals
  • Venue partnerships: official pickup zones at stadiums and arenas
  • Pre-landing grouping: match forms mid-flight, ready at the gate
  • Second and third airport, same playbook
PHASE 3 · SCALE

Every arrival hall

  • Recruit our own drivers at airports and venues where pools already form daily
  • Airline and booking-site partnerships (verify at checkout)
  • Dynamic pricing that fills cars without surge games
  • Corporate and crew travel pools
  • International rollout, starting where the pain is worst
How do you verify a PNR without airline APIs?

Airlines don't offer open PNR lookups, so we don't ask them. You scan your boarding pass (on-device OCR reads name, flight and date) or forward your booking-confirmation email, which we parse to the same fields. Either proves a real booking in under ten seconds — and the PNR itself is deleted right after verification.

My flight landed late. Do I lose my group?

No. Landpool tracks live flight status, so a delayed arrival slides your window forward and you land in the next pool headed your way. The group chat updates everyone automatically — no re-booking, no curb-side panic.

Why not just match people on my own flight?

Because one flight rarely fills a car to the same neighborhood. Matching across every flight landing within ±45 minutes makes the pool 3–5× bigger, so groups form in minutes instead of maybe. Your PNR is the trust layer; the time window is the matching layer.

Is this just Uber Pool at the airport?

Shared airport shuttles (like Uber Shuttle at JFK, ~$18 vs $60–80 solo) proved travelers will share. What nobody has built is the social layer: verified fellow passengers, an invite-only group, private chat and calls, and one agreed meeting point. That's the part Landpool adds — and it runs no cars of its own: the group books the ride on Uber or Lyft, exactly like today.

Who actually drives?

Nobody from Landpool — at first. The platform's job is arranging the people: your group forms, chats privately, and agrees on a meeting point, then books one regular Uber or Lyft together and splits the fare in that app. The driver never joins your chat. If the pools become a hit, a later phase recruits our own vetted drivers — matching comes first, driving comes second.

Does this work for concerts and games?

Yes — that's Concert mode. Swap the PNR for your event ticket, the arrival window for the ±45 minutes around the show's end, and the terminal pillar for the venue's official pickup zone. Flip the Airport / Concert toggle at the top of this page and the whole demo — board, chat, savings — switches with it.

What does it cost?

Riders split the actual fare; Landpool takes a small booking fee per seat. With four riders, a $72 solo ride lands around $19 each including the fee — less than half the solo price even before anyone's time is counted.
